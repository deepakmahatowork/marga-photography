The morning air along the Geruwa River is heavy with dew and the scent of wild elephant grass. Unlike the open savannahs of Africa, where wildlife can be spotted from miles away, tracking in Bardia is a multisensory discipline of listening, waiting, and matching the rhythm of the jungle.

Our naturalist tracker stood motionless on the sandy bank, his finger raised to caution absolute stillness. Three hundred yards downstream, a spotted deer barked its high-pitched warning. Seconds later, a flock of parakeets burst from the canopy in screaming flight.

Then, emerging from the tall cane as if born of the mist itself, the Royal Bengal Tiger stepped silently into the shallows. The early sun caught the golden rim of its muscular shoulders as water rippled around its paws. For four breathless minutes, we captured a majestic testament to Nepal's world-leading conservation triumph.
